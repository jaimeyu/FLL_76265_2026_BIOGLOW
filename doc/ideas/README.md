## 🌊 The 2026 Innovation Challenge

> **The Challenge:** Teams must explore how technology, creativity, and research can help us better understand and protect natural habitats. Inspired by nature, students are asked to identify a specific problem related to ecosystems and develop an innovative solution using their STEM skills.

### 🧭 The 5-Phase Universal Innovation Framework for Students

1. **Phase 1: Identify (Specific Habitat Problem)**:
   - Don't just say "save the ocean" — pick a specific habitat (coral reef, deep abyss, kelp forest, mangrove, polar ice, estuary) and an exact living organism.
   - Describe what is harming them, the root cause, and why existing tools struggle to see or solve it.
2. **Phase 2: Design (Biomimicry & Decision Matrix)**:
   - Nature has 3.8 billion years of engineering R&D! Brainstorm multiple ideas inspired by natural superpowers.
   - Use a team Decision Matrix to objectively compare and select the winning solution.
3. **Phase 3: Create (Tangible Representation)**:
   - Build a representation to prove the engineering concept: LEGO Technic prototype, cardboard scale mockup, or 3D CAD model.
   - *Note:* A fully functional, production-ready machine is NOT required—judges evaluate the engineering design process!
4. **Phase 4: Iterate (The "Share" Rule)**:
   - Share the proposed solution (V1.0) with domain experts (marine biologists, park rangers, engineers, or real-world users).
   - Gather critical constructive feedback and engineer **Version 2.0** to fix the flaws identified!
5. **Phase 5: Communicate (The 5-Minute Pitch)**:
   - Choreograph an engaging 5-minute presentation where **100% of team members speak** and demonstrate the prototype.
   - Master closed-loop Q&A using Crew Resource Management (CRM).
