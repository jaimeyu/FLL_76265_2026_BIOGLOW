FIRST FLL #76265 LEGO LEGENDS
Compass: "Not all obstacles are in your control, but your responses to obstacles are in your control."
Field Rule: 👐 Hands in Pockets & Eyes on the Kids!
1. Inspire: Who am I elevating today who feels nervous or overlooked?
2. Recognize: Celebrate poise, kindness, and recovery above scoreboards.
3. Response: Calm tone, affirmative body language, zero mentor tinkering.
1. 🎪 Morning Pit Arrival & Sensory Staging (07:30 – 08:30) Staging & Baseline
Low-Pressure, Inclusive Arrival Greetings FIRST Tip
Do not force handshakes or high-fives; offer choices (verbal check-in, fist bump, peace sign, or wave). Respect physical autonomy.
"Good morning team! I'm Coach Sam (he/him). Super excited to fly with everyone today!"
Respect Eye Contact & Processing Differences FIRST Tip
Nervousness, cultural differences, and neurodivergence can make direct eye contact uncomfortable. Never equate lack of eye contact with disrespect. Listen attentively regardless of gaze.
Establish Pit "Decompression Corner"
Set up a quiet chair/zone with ear defenders or notebooks for any teammate facing sensory overload in noisy arenas.
Hardware & Checklist Staging (Kids Execute, Mentors Observe)
Verify 2 laminated Run Card sets secured to lanyards; alignment jigs checked; battery 100% charged with spare ready.
2. 🧠 Judging Room Staging (Innovation, Robot Design & Core Values) Judging Prep
Pre-Judging Centering Huddle (15 Min Before)
Lead 4x4 Box Breathing in the hallway. Affirm the crew: "Judges are volunteers excited to hear your story. You own this!"
Zero Omission & Unconscious Bias Check FIRST Tip
Ensure all students speak on technical mechanisms, code, and biomimicry. Never divide roles along gender or vocal stereotypes.
Silent Mentor Presence & Patient Processing FIRST Tip
Mentors sit silently in back chairs. Zero hand signals or whispers. Give students patience and processing time during Judge Q&A.
Immediate Post-Judging Plus'ing Circle
As soon as the door closes, celebrate team bravery and cohesive speaking before dissecting individual answers.
3. 🤝 Volunteer & Referee Interactions (Gracious Professionalism®) Volunteer Comms
Model Deep Respect for Event Volunteers
Greet queuers, referees, and resetters with genuine appreciation. Event days are long and exhausting for all volunteers.
Answer Repetitive Questions with Fresh Grace FIRST Tip
Even if asked the same question 5 times, answer as if for the very first time. Mind translation and terminology barriers.
Referee Rule Discussions: Kids-First Protocol
Mentors never argue with referees. Only table Technicians discuss scoring before signing. Model gracious acceptance.
Technician to Ref: "Thank you, Ref! Could you clarify how rule M04 was scored?"
4. 🤖 Robot Game Field Operations (2.5-Minute Match Cycle) Live Table Run
Strict "Hands in Pockets" Behind Perimeter Line
Mentors keep hands buried in pockets. No tactical barking. Only positive cheers ("Great recovery!", "You got this!").
Closed-Loop CRM Callouts Monitoring
Support student callouts: "Check Program!" → "Program 1 Confirmed!" → "Jig Set!" → "Clear to Launch?"
Support "Tap Out" Protocol & Calm Touch Penalties
If a technician calls "Tap Out", teammate swaps in Home with zero drama. Remind kids: 1 touch penalty is worth saving 25 seconds!
3-Minute Post-Match Debrief Ritual (Away from Table)
1. Best CRM moment? 2. What surprised our system? 3. How do we plus the jig or checklist? (Zero finger-pointing).
5. 🔋 Afternoon Stamina, Sensory Load & Burnout Prevention Fatigue Protocol
Detect Biological Sensory Fatigue FIRST Tip
Watch for slumping, tears, withdrawal, or irritability around 13:00–15:00. Respond with hydration, snacks, and decompression.
Rotate Away from the Pit
Send kids in pairs on scouting missions or to relax outside the gym to reset their sensory thresholds.
💬 The 6 Micro-Affirmations Tournament Matrix FIRST® Guidance
Channel Positive Micro-Affirmation (✅ DO THIS) Negative Micro-Message (❌ AVOID)
1. Verbal "Engineers", "Technicians", gender-neutral language. "Come on guys!", referring to coders as "he/him".
2. Para-Verbal Calm, measured, respectful, unhurried tone. Sounding stressed, annoyed, or sharp.
3. Non-Verbal Hands in pockets, gentle smile, eye-level posture. Pacing, wringing hands, staring at ref with arms crossed.
4. Contextual "Who is launching Run 2?", let kids talk to judges. Assuming who drives; talking over students.
5. Omission Attentive listening to quiet or hesitant students. Only engaging the loudest team members.
6. Praise Equitable praise: girls on code/torque; boys on teamwork/docs. Complimenting girls only on looks and boys on tech.
